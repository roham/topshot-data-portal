The first-principle unlock

Every comp — Card Ladder, the NFT analytics sites — shows you prices and charts: the what. But NBA digital collectibles have something physical cards and most NFT dashboards can't surface: total on-chain transparency of the people and the flow — who owns what, who's accumulating, who's dumping, the full provenance of every serial. The moat isn't a prettier floor chart. It's making the living market of named collectors visible, social, and explorable. The terminal should feel alive — a trading floor — not a spreadsheet.

Landing is the market breathing in real time: a cinematic pulse of what's trading right now, who is buying and selling (real handles), the biggest moves, accumulation vs distribution by the L/XL collectors — with macro cap context ambient behind it. Browse is organized around activity + actors.

Arresting because: depth + a provenance graph nobody else can render. Beats: Bloomberg × Wikipedia × on-chain provenance. The most "encyclopedic."

Don't pick one and discard the rest — sequence them. Lead with A's living-market energy as the arrival moment (it's the true differentiator and the thing no comp has), give it B's museum-grade data-viz as the macro backdrop, and make C's entity + provenance model the browsing backbone underneath.
